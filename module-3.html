Website Structure Planning

Learn how to turn a business goal into a clear website structure — including pages, navigation, categories, user journeys and a sitemap.

Pages

Navigation

02 — Concept

What Is Website Structure?

Website structure is the way the pages, content, categories and navigation of a website are organized.

Think of a website like a building.

Before constructing a building, we need a plan. We decide where the entrance, rooms, corridors and other areas should be located.

A website works in a similar way. Before creating pages, we decide what pages are required, how they are connected and how visitors will move between them.

A basic website structure

Business Goal → Pages → Navigation → Categories → User Journey → Sitemap

03 — Planning

Business Goal & Target Audience

The website structure should begin with the business goal and the people who will use the website.

Business Goal

Ask: What should the website help the business achieve?

  • Generate enquiries
  • Sell products
  • Get bookings
  • Build brand awareness
  • Provide information
  • Generate leads

Target Audience

Ask: Who is the website being created for?

  • Age group
  • Location
  • Needs
  • Problems
  • Buying behaviour
  • Information they are looking for

Example

Business: Local Fashion Boutique

Goal: Showcase products and generate online enquiries.

Audience: Women looking for sarees, kurtis and accessories.

04 — Pages

Planning Website Pages

Every website does not need the same pages. Choose pages according to the business goal and visitor requirements.

Home

Introduces the business and guides visitors to important sections.

About

Explains the business, brand, people or story.

Products / Services

Shows the products or services offered.

Blog

Provides useful content and supports content marketing and SEO.

Contact

Gives visitors a way to contact the business.

Privacy Policy

Provides important information about privacy and data handling.

05 — Website Components

Header, Footer & CTA

Important website elements should help visitors understand where they are and what they can do next.

Header

Usually contains the logo, main navigation, important links and sometimes a primary CTA.

Logo → Home → About → Products → Contact

Footer

Provides supporting links, contact details, social links and legal pages.

Contact → Social → Privacy → Copyright

CTA

A Call To Action tells the visitor what action they can take next.

Shop Now · Book Now · Contact Us · Enquire
06 — Navigation

Planning Website Navigation

Navigation helps users move from one page or section to another without getting confused.

Good navigation should be:

  • Simple
  • Consistent
  • Easy to understand
  • Relevant to the website goal
  • Easy to use on mobile devices

09 — Sitemap

What Is a Sitemap?

A sitemap is a visual or structured representation of the pages and hierarchy of a website.

Important distinction

In this module, students first learn the concept of a website structure sitemap — a planning document showing pages and relationships.

Later, in the SEO module, you will learn about the XML sitemap used by search engines.

11 — Student Practical

Plan Your Own Website Structure

PRACTICAL 01

Define the Business Goal & Target Audience

Start with the business/project you are already working on.

  • Write the business name.
  • Identify the main business goal.
  • Define the target audience.
  • Write what the visitor should do on the website.
Deliverable: One-page Business Goal & Target Audience note.
PRACTICAL 02

List Website Pages & Navigation

Decide which pages your website needs.

  • List the required pages.
  • Choose which pages appear in the main navigation.
  • Decide whether any page belongs under another page.
  • Keep the navigation simple and clear.
Deliverable: Page list + planned navigation menu.
PRACTICAL 03

Build Categories & Subcategories

If your website contains products, services or content groups, organize them into categories.

  • Create main categories.
  • Add relevant subcategories if required.
  • Avoid unnecessary categories.
  • Make category names easy for visitors to understand.
Deliverable: Category and subcategory structure.
PRACTICAL 04

Map the User Journey

Select one important visitor goal and map the path from landing on the website to completing the action.

  • Where does the visitor enter?
  • What page do they visit next?
  • What information do they need?
  • What CTA should they see?
  • What is the final desired action?
Example: Home → Products → Product → Enquire
PRACTICAL 05

Create Your Website Sitemap

Combine everything you planned into one visual website structure.

  • Start with Home.
  • Add the main pages.
  • Add categories and subcategories.
  • Show the page hierarchy clearly.
  • Make sure the sitemap matches your navigation.
Deliverable: One complete sitemap for your project.
