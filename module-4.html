02 — Concept

03 — E-Commerce Models

B2B, B2C & D2C

Different businesses use e-commerce in different ways depending on who sells to whom.

B2B

Business to Business

A business sells products or services to another business.

Example: A mobile spare-parts supplier selling parts to mobile repair shops.

B2C

Business to Consumer

A business sells directly to individual customers.

Example: A fashion store selling clothes to individual customers.

D2C

Direct to Consumer

A brand sells its own products directly to consumers without relying mainly on traditional retail intermediaries.

Example: A brand selling its own products through its own online store.

06 — Categories

Categories & Subcategories

Categories organize products so customers can browse the store more easily.

Example — Fashion Store

  • Women
  • Sarees
  • Kurtis
  • Churidars
  • Accessories

Category planning rule

Categories should help customers find products. Avoid creating many unnecessary categories simply to make the store look larger.

07 — Product Types

Simple & Variable Products

Products can be structured differently depending on whether customers need to choose variations.

Simple Product

A product that does not require customers to select variations before purchasing.

Example: Black Leather Wallet — ₹699

Variable Product

A product that has variations such as size, colour or other selectable attributes.

Example: T-Shirt — Size S / M / L + Colours

Payment Gateway Concept

A payment gateway is a service that enables online payment processing between a customer, the store and the payment system.

For classroom practice, use a suitable test/sandbox payment environment whenever possible. Do not use real customer card or payment information for practice.

PRACTICAL 04

Create Simple & Variable Products

Create different product types so that you can understand how product configuration changes.

  • Create at least one simple product.
  • Create one variable product if your project requires it.
  • Add attributes such as size or colour.
  • Test the variations on the product page.
Deliverable: Product screenshots showing the product type and available variations.
PRACTICAL 05

Test Product → Cart → Checkout

Perform a complete test purchase using your practice store.

  • Open a product.
  • Add the product to the cart.
  • Change quantity if applicable.
  • Open the cart.
  • Proceed to checkout.
  • Enter test customer details.
  • Complete the test order using an appropriate test method.
Deliverable: Screenshots showing Product → Cart → Checkout → Order confirmation.
PRACTICAL 06

Manage the Order from Admin

After placing the test order, return to the WordPress dashboard.

  • Open WooCommerce Orders.
  • Find the test order.
  • Review customer and product information.
  • Check the order status.
  • Update the order status.
Deliverable: Screenshot of the test order inside the WordPress/WooCommerce dashboard.
